Procure-to-pay is where enterprises quietly lose millions of hours. One purchase crosses a dozen disconnected systems — Coupa, Oracle, SharePoint, Remedy — and a chain of hand-offs: raise the requisition, onboard the supplier, sign the contract, cut the PO, receive goods, match the invoice, clear holds, pay, accrue, and report. Every step is a form, an email, an approval, or a wait.

One conversation, from requisition to accrual.

Two things make this usable, not just clever.

First, it converges. If you ask for a purchase order before a contract exists, the assistant does not simply stop. It tells you which cases are ready and what you need to do next.

Second, control stays with people. Every real decision stops for human approval, and every decision is written to the governance log.

00:03:14
click "Architecture" and "scroll down and up slowly"

This isn't a toy. The agents call Coupa, Oracle, SharePoint, and Remedy through the same interfaces used by the real systems, so production endpoints can be configured without changing the overall workflow.

State lives in a shared datastore, so a conversation can survive a restart and scale across multiple replicas.

Long-running approvals are modeled as durable workflows, with a fallback so the assistant never simply stops.
